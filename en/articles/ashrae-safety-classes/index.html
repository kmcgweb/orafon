What do A1, A2L and A3 mean? Understanding refrigerant safety classes

The ASHRAE 34 safety code shows both the toxicity and flammability of a refrigerant, helping you choose and install it safely.

22 September 2026

Every refrigerant grade has a safety classification under ASHRAE 34, made of one letter and one number.

The letter: toxicity

  • A — lower toxicity
  • B — higher toxicity; requires ventilation and leak detection

The number: flammability

  • 1 — no flame propagation
  • 2L — lower flammability; hard to ignite with a slow burning velocity
  • 2 — flammable
  • 3 — higher flammability

Common examples

Class Examples
A1 R-22, R-134a, R-410A, R-404A, R-448A
A2L R-32, R-454B, R-1234yf
A2 R-152a, R-141b
A3 R-290 (Propane), R-600a (Isobutane)
B1 R-245fa

What technicians should know

  • A2L and A3 refrigerants may only be used in equipment designed for them — never as a replacement in systems designed for A1 refrigerants.
  • A3 refrigerants have maximum charge and installation-space limits; they should be handled by trained technicians.
  • Always check storage and handling details in the product SDS.

SDS documents for ORAFON and Solstice® products are available under TDS/SDS documents.
